01 / The challenge

Prevention starts with clear information

For decades, NASA has conducted experiments to study fire in microgravity. However, results can be scattered and difficult to locate, compare, and interpret.

01

Scattered information

Scientific data is spread across multiple studies and sets of results.

02

Complex comparisons

Finding similar experiments and relating their conditions can be time-consuming.

03

Practical safety insights

Scientific findings need to be turned into understandable information that supports preventive decisions.

Why does it matter? In microgravity, the absence of natural buoyancy changes how hot gases move. Flames can become roughly spherical, creating challenges for detecting, understanding, and extinguishing fires.
02 / Our solution

From experiments to prevention

NASA Fire Intelligence proposes organizing space-combustion information and making it easier to analyze through an interactive platform.

01

Data collection

Gather NASA combustion research and experiments.

02

Organization

Classify by fuel, temperature, oxygen, airflow, and experimental conditions.

03

Processing

Clean, standardize, and structure the data.

04

Intelligent analysis

Use AI and statistical methods to identify patterns and potential risks.

05

Visualization

Present results, interpretations, and recommendations in a dashboard.

AI capabilities: summarize research, identify similarities, connect variables, classify combustion conditions, and generate evidence-based explanations. The platform would be a scientific support tool, not a replacement for official safety procedures.
03 / Applications

Safety for future missions

Organized information can support research, design, and decision-making related to fire prevention and suppression.

🌕

Moon

Support material selection and habitat design using improved fire-safety criteria.

🔴

Mars

Help analyze risks on long-duration missions, where evacuation and resupply are more complex.

🛰️

Spacecraft and stations

Improve understanding of fire detection, spread, and suppression in enclosed environments.
